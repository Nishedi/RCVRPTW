\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{I}{n} an era of rapidly changing supply chains, demand is growing for flexible transportation services that serve store networks, pickup points, construction sites, and temporary events. Transport companies perform daily deliveries of materials and components whose sizes may vary from day to day. Customers typically specify acceptable delivery and unloading time windows (e.g., for safety or operational reasons), so each visit must not only occur at a scheduled time but also complete unloading within a designated time window.

Time windows need not be static — they can be modified due to delays, demand fluctuations, or customer requests. In such conditions, planning methods capable of maintaining solution quality under changing input data are particularly important. Although the approach presented in this work does not constitute a formal robust optimization method in the theoretical sense, it demonstrates characteristics of \emph{operational robustness}. The use of multiple stochastic scenario analysis additionally allows assessment of how algorithm behavior changes depending on perturbations, thereby enabling a more comprehensive evaluation of its stability. The algorithm's ability to intelligently explore the solution space means it adapts better to disruptions than constructive approaches, which enables reduction of operational costs and decreased risk of time window violations under uncertainty.

Taking into account the fleet's limited capacity and service times that depend on delivery size and unloading conditions, route planning and load allocation become a combinatorial problem burdened with many realistic operational constraints: vehicle capacities (capacitated), time windows of availability, and variable demand at delivery points. In this paper, we propose a mathematical model that reflects the described conditions and compare two solution methods: a greedy approach and a tailored metaheuristic adapted to large instances. An additional value of this work is the ability to observe to what extent these methods differ in terms of robustness to uncertainty and how they behave under extreme conditions. We conduct experiments on benchmark data extended with stochastic modifications of customer time windows and demands.

\subsection{Research hypotheses}
To evaluate the effectiveness and stability under uncertainty of the proposed approaches, we formulate the following research hypotheses:

\begin{itemize}
    \item[\textbf{H1.}] The metaheuristic (Tabu Search) provides a lower expected total cost than the greedy algorithm across a broad set of stochastic scenarios.
    
    \item[\textbf{H2.}] The Tabu Search exhibits smaller variability of results across scenarios, which indicates higher stability and operational robustness with respect to changing input data.
    
    \item[\textbf{H3.}] The Tabu Search minimizes the total penalty associated with time-window violations compared to the greedy approach. While in some configurations avoiding penalties may be impossible, the goal is to reduce their magnitude.
    
    \item[\textbf{H4.}] The Tabu Search reduces the loss tail — that is, the frequency and severity of extreme-cost scenarios. Fewer extreme cases indicate greater reliability of the method under difficult operating conditions.
    
\end{itemize}

The above hypotheses address not only average solution quality but also their stability and reliability under uncertainty. This allows the analysis to move beyond nominal optimization results and assess algorithm behavior in the presence of stochastic disturbances — a crucial factor in practical logistics applications.
